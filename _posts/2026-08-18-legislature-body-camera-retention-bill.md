A bill establishing a uniform minimum retention period for law
enforcement body-worn camera footage has cleared its committee vote and is
headed to the floor for a full chamber vote. If passed, the measure would
standardize retention timelines that currently vary significantly from
agency to agency across Kansas.

## Key provisions

The bill, as currently drafted, would require:

- A baseline retention period for all non-evidentiary footage.
- An extended retention requirement for footage tied to a use-of-force incident, complaint, or open investigation.
- A standardized process for footage requested under the Kansas Open Records Act.

Supporters argue a consistent statewide floor reduces confusion for
agencies operating across county lines and for the public requesting
footage. Some smaller agencies have raised concerns about the cost of
expanded digital storage required to meet the new minimums.

## What agencies should watch

> "Even if this doesn't pass this session, it's worth reviewing your
> agency's current retention policy now — the direction of travel is
> pretty clear," noted a county attorney who has tracked similar bills in
> neighboring states.

Agency leadership and records custodians should:

1. Review current body-worn camera retention schedules against the proposed minimums.
2. Estimate storage cost impact if extended retention becomes mandatory.
3. Flag any policy conflicts between local retention schedules and the proposed statewide floor.

## Legislative timeline

The bill must clear a floor vote in its chamber of origin before crossing
over. KLEN will continue tracking the bill's status and will publish an
update if it advances further or is amended.
